# Introduction (generic contract)

A personal layer (house format, quotas, forbidden lists) overrides this file. This file states what a defensible Introduction contains, whichever house style applies.

Aligned with A `05_manuscript` Introduction v3 (six elements, claim coverage, Frontiers/graylist are 选刊-side only).

## Elements

1. Disease and burden.
2. Guideline definition: the latest edition of the guideline that defines the endpoint or the clinical decision, paraphrased, with the decision that rests on it.
3. Limits of current practice, mined from what the guideline leaves open and from the tests it relies on.
4. Related studies: the main results of the two or three most relevant (n, design, effect), then a group appraisal of one or two sentences: the strength the study keeps and the weakness it targets.
5. Missing piece: one open question stated as an unknown, then one hedged sentence on why the study's method may address it.
6. Aim and hypothesis: no citations; one endpoint name; the set and the comparator named.

## Writing rules

- Sentences of 10–30 words, one main proposition each.
- Synthesize; do not list. No run of sentences that each report one study. At most one triplet per paragraph. No repeated sentence openers.
- Scarcity is worded as what a logged search found ("in the studies we identified"), not as what exists.
- Inference about why a method may help is allowed. Hedge it, and keep the gap → method → expected benefit → measured output map in a comment.
- Reporting guidelines (TRIPOD, CLAIM, CLEAR) belong to the checklist and the submission form, not to the Introduction. Route to `05-write-reporting`.
- Numbers only from sources read at abstract level or above; guideline definitions and appraisals from the full text.
- Missing evidence → an Evidence Request card (`missing_prior_result`, `guideline_definition`, `related_work_appraisal`, `scarcity_check`), never a guessed citation.
