**Canonical entry:** `../SKILL.md` (manuscript-core). Mode: `de-ai` / polish.

## Academic override (mandatory for SCI manuscripts)

Stop-slop was written for general prose. For Ying Li imaging SCI:

1. **Do not** insert colloquial "you" / room-narrator voice into Methods/Results/Discussion.
2. **Methods** may stay predominantly **passive** (lab house style) — do not force active voice in Methods.
3. **Never** change numbers, P, CI, HR/OR, units, citations, or ethics IDs.
4. Prefer structural burstiness (sentence length mix) over informal register.
5. Em-dash: prediction-model full papers forbid em-dash (use mode-1-sci); other sections minimize AI-style em-dash stacks.
6. Forbidden academic fluff is owned in manuscript-core hard rules + `forbidden-phrases.md`.

**Below this line is the original general-prose stop-slop ruleset, kept for reference.**
**Where it conflicts with the override above, the override wins — do not apply Core Rule 3
(active voice) to Methods, and do not apply Core Rule 7 (skip softening) to Discussion hedges.
Items 1/5/8 below and most of `phrases.md`/`structures.md`/`examples.md` target informal/blog
prose (e.g. "Plot twist:", "circle back") that does not occur in medical SCI writing — treat
them as background reference, not active rules, unless polishing non-manuscript text.**

# Stop Slop

Eliminate predictable AI writing patterns from prose.

## Core Rules

1. **Cut filler phrases.** Remove throat-clearing openers and emphasis crutches (adverbs like "really"/"simply" are fine in calibrated hedges — see override #2/#6). See [references/phrases.md](phrases.md).

2. **Break formulaic structures.** Avoid binary contrasts, negative listings, dramatic fragmentation, rhetorical setups, false agency. See [references/structures.md](structures.md).

3. **Prefer active voice outside Methods.** In Introduction/Discussion/Conclusion, give sentences a clear actor where doing so doesn't distort meaning. **Methods stays passive per the override above** — do not rewrite Methods sentences to add a human subject.

4. **Be specific.** No vague declaratives ("The reasons are structural") outside calibrated hedges. Name the specific thing. No lazy extremes ("every," "always," "never") doing vague work.

5. **Avoid narrator-from-a-distance filler in prose framing** (not applicable to Methods/Results, which are inherently third-person and impersonal by house style).

6. **Vary rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths where the science allows it. Two items beat three for stylistic lists (not for enumerated inclusion/exclusion criteria). End paragraphs differently. No em dashes.

7. **State results directly; hedge only where evidence requires it.** In Results, state findings directly. In Discussion, calibrated hedges (may/might/could/suggest) are house style, not "softening" — do not cut them.

8. **Cut quotables.** If a sentence reads like a pull-quote or empty flourish, rewrite it plainly.

## Quick Checks

Before delivering prose:

- Any adverbs outside calibrated hedges? Kill them.
- Passive voice **outside Methods**? Consider making the actor the subject; leave Methods passive.
- Inanimate thing doing a human verb ("the decision emerges")? Name the actor, unless it's standard passive-voice Methods phrasing.
- Any "here's what/this/that" throat-clearing? Cut to the point.
- Any "not X, it's Y" contrasts? State Y directly.
- Three consecutive sentences match length? Break one (skip for enumerated criteria/lists).
- Em-dash anywhere? Remove it (mandatory for prediction-model full papers; minimize elsewhere).
- Vague declarative ("The implications are significant")? Name the specific implication.
- Discussion hedge (may/might/could/suggest)? Keep — this is not a vague declarative.
