## Habits seen in Discussion / conclusion cards

1. **Bridge with Therefore / Thus / 因此** after a concrete observation, then one clinical or mechanistic claim — still hedged when evidence is associative.
2. **Classify with explicit criteria** in one sentence (e.g. solid vs denser portions; inclusion cutoffs) rather than vague “types”.
3. **Mechanism language stays calibrated**: “might suggest”, “提示…可能”, “与…相符” — not “proves” / “demonstrates that X causes Y” unless the design supports it.
4. **Limitation sentences are factual**, not CYA essays: retrospective design, parameter inconsistency (e.g. DWI b-value mix), missing standard planes — state the limit once, then what was / was not modeled.
5. **Regional / subgroup differences** get a short anatomic or pathway reason when the data show it; otherwise stop at the finding.
6. **Model / control caveats** named when the animal or toxin model itself could confound (e.g. TAA vs diet-stable models) — still evidence-tied, not generic disclaimer.
7. **Imaging intensity / phase language** ties signal change to a biologic stage (e.g. extracellular methemoglobin) when that is the paper’s claim chain.
8. **English Discussion closer pattern**: “Therefore, in our study, we …” + operational definition; or “We assumed it might suggest that …” for soft inference.
