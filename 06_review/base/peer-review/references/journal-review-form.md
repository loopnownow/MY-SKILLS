# Journal review form, severity tiers, and located comments

Open this file when the journal supplies a review form, when the journal expects severity
tiers, or when a disagreement has to be argued rather than asserted.

## Review form

Answer every field `Yes`, `No`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A blank field reads as "this was not
reviewed" and the editor will ask again. The common eight fields are:

1. English language quality
2. Figures and tables (legibility, labelling, redundancy)
3. Reference list adequate and unbiased
4. Statistical methods valid and correctly used (sample size, choice of test)
5. Methods documented well enough to replicate
6. Data availability (a justified `NOT_APPLICABLE` is an answer, silence is not)
7. Ethics approval and participant consent
8. Biosecurity and institutional safety

Answer the field first, then attach one sentence of reason. A `No` without a located reason is
not usable by the editor.

## Severity tiers

| Tier | Label | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Tier 1 (Essential): Must be completed for acceptance` | Blocks acceptance; maps to a Major comment |
| 2 | `Tier 2 (Strongly Recommended)` | Major when it moves the main claim, otherwise Minor |
| 3 | `Tier 3 (Optional but Impactful)` | Minor or suggestion |

Do not invent a fourth tier, and do not label a cosmetic item Tier 1 to sound severe. When the
journal has no tier scheme, the Major / Minor split carries the same information.

## Comment schemas

Ordinary item, in one paragraph or three short fields:

    Location: section, page or line
    Risk: the guideline item, statistic, or reporting rule it breaks
    Required action: what to do, and where

Contested interpretation, only when the authors' reading of their own data is the issue:

    Claim n: one-line statement of the claim
    Authors' Interpretation: the quoted sentence
    Challenge: the evidence that undercuts it (PMID, statistic, guideline item)
    Recommendation: the bounded action

The second schema is too heavy for nits; keep it for items that decide the recommendation.

## Two report skeletons

Skeleton A, form-driven journals: 2-3 sentence recap, then strengths, then the ticked form,
then numbered questions.

Skeleton B, narrative journals: 2-3 sentence recap, then `Strengths.` 1) 2) 3), then
`Limitations` 1) 2), then numbered questions.

In both, the recap is descriptive (what the study did, what it claims), never evaluative, and
strengths come before weaknesses. Reviewers who skip the recap tend to misread the claim and
write comments the authors cannot act on.

## Layering editor and reviewer

Editor comments get their own section (`Replies to Editor #1`, `#2`). Each reviewer gets
`Reviewer #n`, own section, own numbering. Never merge the two layers into a single block, and
never let one reviewer's numbering continue another's.

## One file per reviewer

When a round contains several reviewers, keep one file per reviewer (`R<n>__Reviewer <i>`).
Merging them into one file destroys the record that the opinions formed independently.

## Recurring compliance nits

These are raised once, in the right section, with the location:

- a validation arm too small for the claim, with the number stated rather than "small"
- reference style: target journal output style, bracketed numbers not superscripts, all
  authors listed, `and` before the last author, no DOI/PMID/PMCID in the list, PubMed journal
  abbreviations
- figure lettering below print size
- an abstract split into subheadings when the journal requires one paragraph
- a table that no sentence in the text cites

## Never

- Merge comments and responses into a submitted review file; that shape is archive-only.
- State a recommendation in the submitted letter when the form has no field for it.
- Rely on, quote, or anticipate another reviewer's report.
- Copy author identifiers (names, affiliations, email addresses, ethics numbers) into a skill body,
  a harvest note, or a shared draft.
- Add an AI-use disclosure to a review unless the user explicitly asks for one.
